# Camera Shot Vocabulary — for MiniMax H3 R2V prompts

Source: StudioBinder "50+ Types of Camera Shots, Angles, and Techniques"
(https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/ultimate-guide-to-camera-shots/)

Purpose: a complete cheatsheet of shot size / framing / focus / angle / movement /
mechanism terminology, with ready-to-paste English phrases for MiniMax H3 R2V
prompts. Adding these precise camera terms to a MiniMax prompt gives much better
shot control than vague words like "camera moves".

RULE: in a MiniMax R2V prompt, name the shot size + angle + movement + mechanism
EXPLICITLY for every [Shot N] (e.g. "a static eye-level medium shot", "a slow
tracking shot from the side", "a low-angle shot looking up"). MiniMax responds to
standard cinematography vocabulary.

====================================================================
1. SHOT SIZE (how much of the subject fills the frame)
====================================================================
Abbreviation | What it frames | MiniMax phrase to paste
-------------|----------------|--------------------------
EWS / ELS    | subject tiny against the location, lots of environment | "an extreme wide shot, the subject appears very small against the vast location"
WS / LS      | whole body in view, still not filling the frame, space above and below | "a wide shot, the subject's whole body in view with plenty of space around them"
FS           | head to toe, subject fills the frame but some scenery stays | "a full shot, the subject's entire body from head to toe, filling most of the frame"
MWS / MLS    | roughly the knees up | "a medium wide shot, framing the subject from roughly the knees up"
CS (cowboy)  | roughly mid-thighs up | "a cowboy shot, framing the subject from roughly mid-thighs up"
MS           | roughly the waist up, through the torso | "a medium shot, framing the subject from roughly the waist up"
MCU          | roughly the chest up, favors the face | "a medium close-up, framing the subject from roughly the chest up"
CU           | a part of the subject fills the frame (usually the face) | "a close-up, the subject's face filling the frame"
ECU          | the most you can fill the frame (eyes, mouth, a small object) | "an extreme close-up, filling the frame with a single detail"
EST          | head of a scene, shows the location/geography (usually wide) | "an establishing shot, clearly showing the location of the action"

Jaisal Cut note: we use MEDIUM SHOTS ONLY (MS) — the figure's full body visible,
small in the frame. NO wide/establishing shots, NO close-ups.

====================================================================
2. SHOT FRAMING (how many subjects, and from whose perspective)
====================================================================
- Single shot — one subject featured. Phrase: "a single shot featuring only one
  character, no other people in the frame".
- Two shot — two subjects together. Phrase: "a two-shot framing two characters
  together".
- Three shot — three subjects. Phrase: "a three-shot framing three characters".
- Over-the-Shoulder (OTS) — from behind another character's shoulder. Phrase:
  "an over-the-shoulder shot, the camera behind one character's shoulder looking
  at the other".
- Over-the-Hip (OTH) — like OTS but from the hip. Phrase: "an over-the-hip shot".
- Point-of-View (POV) — what the character sees. Phrase: "a point-of-view shot,
  showing exactly what the character sees".
- Clean single vs dirty single — clean = one character alone; dirty = background
  extras/foreground obstructions present. Phrase: "a clean single shot, one
  character alone with no other person visible".

Jaisal Cut note: every shot is a CLEAN SINGLE (only the one stick figure).

====================================================================
3. SHOT FOCUS / DEPTH OF FIELD (what is sharp)
====================================================================
- Rack focus / focus pull — focus shifts from one subject to another. Phrase:
  "a rack focus, the focus shifts from the foreground subject to the background".
- Shallow focus (shallow DOF) — subject sharp, foreground+background blurred.
  Phrase: "a shallow focus shot, the subject in crisp focus while the background
  is out of focus".
- Deep focus — everything in the frame is in focus. Phrase: "a deep focus shot,
  everything in the frame is in focus".
- Tilt-shift — selective focus, stylized. Phrase: "a tilt-shift shot".
- Soft focus — nothing 100% sharp, dreamy glow. Phrase: "a soft focus shot, a
  dreamy glow, nothing fully sharp".
- Split diopter — foreground AND background sharp, middle blurred. Phrase: "a
  split diopter shot".

Jaisal Cut note: for a 2D line-art piece, use DEEP FOCUS (everything in the line
drawing is sharp) — phrase: "a deep focus shot, every line in the frame is sharp".

====================================================================
4. CAMERA ANGLE (where the camera is placed)
====================================================================
- Eye level — neutral, camera at the subject's eye height. Phrase: "an eye-level
  shot, the camera at the subject's eye height, a neutral perspective".
- Low angle — camera low, looking UP at the subject (power/superiority). Phrase:
  "a low-angle shot, the camera positioned low looking up at the subject".
- High angle — camera high, looking DOWN at the subject (inferiority). Phrase:
  "a high-angle shot, the camera positioned high looking down at the subject".
- Hip level — camera roughly waist-high. Phrase: "a hip-level shot, the camera
  roughly waist-high".
- Knee level — camera about as low as the subject's knees. Phrase: "a knee-level
  shot, the camera about as low as the subject's knees".
- Ground level — camera on the ground. Phrase: "a ground-level shot, the camera at
  ground level".
- Shoulder level — camera about as high as the subject's shoulders. Phrase: "a
  shoulder-level shot, the camera about as high as the subject's shoulders".
- Dutch angle / dutch tilt — camera slanted to one side (disorientation). Phrase:
  "a dutch angle shot, the camera slanted to one side, the horizon tilted".
- Bird's eye / overhead — from way up high looking straight down. Phrase: "a
  bird's-eye overhead shot, the camera directly above looking straight down".
- Aerial — from way up high (drone/helicopter), establishes scenery. Phrase: "an
  aerial shot from high above, establishing the expanse of scenery".

Jaisal Cut note: the title animation uses EYE-LEVEL medium shots (neutral, the
figure at eye height). For the jump shot, a LOW-ANGLE looking up at the figure can
add drama.

====================================================================
5. CAMERA MOVEMENT (how the camera moves)
====================================================================
- Static / fixed — no movement, locked aim. Phrase: "a static shot, the camera is
  locked and does not move".
- Dolly — camera on a track, moves toward/away. Phrase: "a dolly shot, the camera
  moves smoothly toward the subject on a track".
- Zoom — the LENS changes focal length (camera stays put). Phrase: "a zoom shot,
  the lens zooms in on the subject while the camera stays in place".
- Dolly zoom / vertigo — camera position AND focal length change together (warp).
  Phrase: "a dolly-zoom vertigo shot, the camera moves while the lens zooms,
  creating a warping effect".
- Pan — camera rotates side to side on a horizontal axis. Phrase: "a pan shot, the
  camera rotates side to side".
- Tilt — camera moves up and down on a vertical axis. Phrase: "a tilt shot, the
  camera tilts up and down".
- Whip pan / swish pan — a fast pan creating motion blur (often a transition).
  Phrase: "a whip pan, a fast pan creating motion blur".
- Whip tilt / swish tilt — a fast vertical tilt. Phrase: "a whip tilt, a fast
  vertical tilt creating motion blur".
- Tracking — camera moves WITH the subject (behind/beside). Phrase: "a tracking
  shot, the camera moves alongside the subject, following them".
- Crab — camera moves horizontally like a crab (sideways, staying parallel).
  Phrase: "a crab shot, the camera moves sideways, staying parallel to the
  subject".
- Arc — camera moves around the subject in an arc. Phrase: "an arc shot, the
  camera moves around the subject in a curved arc".

Jaisal Cut note: the walk shot = STATIC or a slow TRACKING shot (camera follows the
figure). The head-turn = the figure turns, camera stays STATIC. The jump = a
STATIC eye-level or LOW-ANGLE shot. Keep it simple — no whip pans, no dolly zooms.

====================================================================
6. CAMERA MECHANISM (the gear that makes the move)
====================================================================
- Sticks / tripod — static shots, simple pans/tilts. Phrase: "shot on a tripod".
- Slider — camera slides on a horizontal/vertical rail. Phrase: "a slider shot,
  the camera glides on a rail".
- Handheld — held by the operator, shaky, gritty. Phrase: "a handheld shot,
  slightly shaky".
- Steadicam — stabilizer, smooth stable moves. Phrase: "a steadicam shot, smooth
  and stable".
- Gimbal — motorized stabilizer, compact. Phrase: "a gimbal shot, smooth and
  stable".
- Crane — sweeps up and over a scene. Phrase: "a crane shot, the camera sweeps up
  and over the scene".
- Jib — like a crane, more limited range. Phrase: "a jib shot".
- Drone — flies over/alongside. Phrase: "a drone shot, the camera flies over the
  scene".
- Wire / cablecam — camera on a cable, smooth deliberate moves. Phrase: "a
  cablecam shot".

Jaisal Cut note: for a 2D line-art animation, the mechanism is implied — just say
"static" or "smooth tracking". Don't name real gear (tripod/dolly) in a 2D
animation prompt; it can confuse the model.

7. READY-TO-PASTE MiniMax H3 R2V SHOT LINES (Jaisal Cut title animation)
====================================================================
Combine: [shot size] + [angle] + [movement] + [focus] + [what happens].

[Shot 1] WALK (medium, static or slow tracking):
"[Shot 1] At 00:00.000, a static eye-level MEDIUM shot (the figure small in the
frame, full body visible), deep focus, the camera locked and not moving: the stick
figure walks SLOWLY along the line-art bridge, his line legs in a slow walking
stride, his line arms swinging gently. About 4 seconds."

[Shot 1b] WALK with a slow tracking move (alternative):
"[Shot 1] At 00:00.000, a slow TRACKING eye-level MEDIUM shot, the camera moves
smoothly alongside the figure at walking pace, deep focus: the stick figure walks
SLOWLY along the line-art bridge. About 4 seconds."

[Shot 2] LOOK AROUND / HEAD TURN (medium, static):
"[Shot 2] At 00:04.000, a static eye-level MEDIUM shot, the camera locked: the
stick figure stops, then TURNS HIS HEAD to look to one side, holds, then TURNS HIS
HEAD to the other side, looking around, then WHISTLES (a small line-art whistle
gesture, one line arm raised). About 4 seconds."

[Shot 3] JUMP (medium, low-angle for drama, static):
"[Shot 3] At 00:08.000, a static LOW-ANGLE MEDIUM shot, the camera positioned low
looking up at the figure, deep focus: the stick figure JUMPS off the edge of the
bridge into the river below, his line body in a mid-air jumping pose, his line
arms out, his line legs bent, the paper plane still flying just ahead of him.
About 4 seconds."

[Shot 4] TITLE (medium, static):
"[Shot 4] At 00:12.000, a static eye-level MEDIUM shot, the camera locked: the
paper plane is still flying over the wavy line water, and the title 'The Jaisal
Cut' (in clean line-art lettering) appears centered in the frame. NO stick figure
in this shot. About 4 seconds."

8. PROMPT-ASSEMBLY TEMPLATE (paste into the MiniMax R2V prompt)
====================================================================
For each [Shot N], write ONE line in this order:
  [Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, a [MOVEMENT] [ANGLE] [SHOT SIZE] shot, [FOCUS]:
  [what the subject does]. About X seconds.

Example full line:
  [Shot 2] At 00:04.000, a static eye-level medium shot, deep focus: the stick
  figure stops, turns his head to look left, then right, then whistles. About 4
  seconds.

Keep the 6-section MiniMax R2V structure (subject_definitions / summary /
retention_analysis / detailed_description / overall_soundscape /
non_diegetic_music) — the camera terms go in the [Shot N] lines of
detailed_description. See MINIMAX_H3_R2V_PROMPTING_GUIDE.md.
