# Episode 1: The Sweetness of What Could Have Been

**Project:** Football & Magical Realism
**Status:** Outline — awaiting user's first-half and second-half scene plans before dialogue writing.

---

## Act I: The Bitter Present

### Scene 1: The Academy Ground (Late Afternoon)

**Setting:** A hard-packed dirt football pitch. Harsh sun. Dust. A group of small kids playing 'Kalapoot' — aggressive fives football. Sliding tackles, shouting, the sound of a leather ball hitting packed earth.

**Characters present:** Zidhan (mid-20s, lean, focused) coaching from the sidelines. Jaisal (16, rose-pink shirt, black trousers, black school bag at his feet) standing apart, leaning against a fence. He is not playing. He watches.

**New character:** Elapan's son (the cousin's son) arrives at the academy. A small kid, maybe 12, with a worn-out football in his hands. He stands at the edge of the pitch, watching the Kalapoot game with wide eyes.

**Action:**
- Zidhan yells instructions. A kid takes a hard fall. Zidhan doesn't help him up — just points to the next play.
- Jaisal's internal monologue (voiceover): "This isn't the game. This is just a fight with a ball."
- Zidhan catches Jaisal's eye. Shakes his head. "You're still dreaming, Jaisal. Dreams don't get you a spot on the roster."
- Jaisal looks away. Not at the game. Through it.

**Beat:** The cousin's son (Elapan's kid) is introduced. Zidhan calls him over, tests him. The kid is natural — instinctive, not trained. Zidhan's eyes flicker: there's something here. But he'll run the kid through Kalapoot drills. Jaisal watches this from the fence, silent.

---

### Scene 2: The Grandfather's Room (Golden Hour)

**Setting:** A small, clean room. A single window. A single beam of sunlight. Abu Bakar (70s, calm eyes) sits on a prayer mat, polishing a vintage mechanical watch with a soft cloth. The room smells of old paper and sandalwood. No music. Just the rhythmic *tick-tick-tick* of the watch.

**Action:**
- Jaisal enters, sits cross-legged across from him.
- Abu Bakar doesn't look up. "You're late for the game. Or early for the next one?"
- Jaisal: "There is no game for me."
- Abu Bakar stops polishing. Holds the watch to the light. "Time is a circle, not a line, beta. Some circles are broken. We just have to find the piece that fits."
- He hands the watch to Jaisal. "Hold it. Feel the weight. Now, tell me: what do you see when you close your eyes and listen to the tick?"

**Beat:** Jaisal closes his eyes. The *tick-tick-tick* becomes the sound of a football hitting the turf. The sunlight in the room shifts, warms, stretches. The edges of the room soften. He's still in the room. He's also somewhere else.

---

## Act II: The Sweet Past (Magical Realism)

### Scene 3: The 'Sweet' Vision

**Setting:** The same academy ground, but transformed. The dirt is lush grass. The kids are the same, but their movements are fluid, joyous. The Kalapoot aggression is gone. It's pure, flowing football. The air is clear. The light is a warm, late-afternoon gold.

**Action:**
- Jaisal is playing. His rose-pink shirt is a vivid spot of color in the green.
- Zidhan is there too, but different — laughing, passing with a grace he doesn't have in the present.
- No dialogue. Just the sound of the game: the thud of the ball, the shouts of joy, the wind in the trees.
- Jaisal scores. The kids cheer. He looks at the sidelines.
- He sees his grandfather, younger, nodding with a proud smile.
- The image is shimmering, slightly unstable at the edges, like a heat haze.

**Beat:** The image flickers. For a second, the Kalapoot version bleeds through — the dust, the hardness, Zidhan's stern face. Then it snaps back. Jaisal grips the watch tighter. The *tick-tick-tick* pulls him back.

---

### Scene 4: The 1950 'What If' Sequence

**Setting:** A shimmering, golden memory of 1950. Not a stadium — a field. The Indian national team, training. The air is different. The light is different. The world feels *possible*.

**Action:**
- Jaisal stands at the edge of the field. He's not playing — he's watching.
- He sees the players who never made it to the World Cup. Their faces are familiar — like his own face, like Zidhan's face, stretched across time.
- The sound of boots on grass. The sound of a ball being passed, passed, passed. Not a fight. A conversation.
- Jaisal's voiceover: "They were here. They were *here*. And then the federation said no. And then the society said no. And then the whole world forgot."
- The golden light flickers. The field starts to dissolve. The players fade.

**Beat:** Jaisal is back in the present. The watch is warm in his hand. The room is the room. Abu Bakar is watching him, a knowing sadness in his eyes.

Abu Bakar (softly): "It's not a dream. It's a memory of a future that never happened. But it's real in the heart."

---

## Act III: The Bitter Present Returns

### Scene 5: The Academy Ground at Night

**Setting:** The same academy ground, but it's night. The game is over. The kids are gone. Only the empty field and a single, buzzing light pole remain. Dust still lingers in the air.

**Action:**
- Zidhan is packing up the gear. He looks at Jaisal, who is still staring at the spot where the 'sweet' game happened.
- Zidhan: "You've been staring at the dirt for ten minutes. What are you seeing?"
- Jaisal: "A game that should have been."
- Zidhan scoffs, but it's not mean. "That's the problem with you. You live in the 'should have been'. I live in the 'is'. And in the 'is', we're out of a league and out of a job."
- He slings the bag over his shoulder. "Come on. I'll buy you tea."
- Jaisal doesn't follow. He stays.

**Final Beat:**
- Jaisal looks at the watch in his hand. He winds it. The ticking starts again.
- He looks at the empty field. Then at Zidhan's retreating back.
- For a moment, the golden light of the 'sweet' past flickers across his face. Just a second.
- The night swallows it.
- He smiles. Small. Sad. Hopeful.
- The episode ends on him: alone on the dark field, holding a ticking watch, dreaming of a game that never was.

## Dialogue Notes (for iterative writing)

- **Jaisal's voice:** Emotional, poetic, but not flowery. He feels the game in his body before his brain. His lines are short when he's in the present, longer when he's in the 'sweet' vision.
- **Zidhan's voice:** Direct, clipped, practical. He doesn't waste words. His materialism isn't cruelty — it's survival. When he's kind, it's understated.
- **Abu Bakar's voice:** Slow, warm, philosophical but not preachy. He speaks in circles, not lines. His lines are the longest, but they land softly.

## Audio Notes

- **NO MUSIC.** Natural ambient sounds only. (Per project rule D041.)
- **Key sounds:** The *tick-tick-tick* of the watch (the only 'rhythmic' element). The thud of a football on dirt. The buzz of the light pole. Wind in the trees. The distant sound of kids playing.
- **The 'sweet' vision** has a different soundscape: the thud of the ball is softer, the grass is audible underfoot, the air is warmer, the kids' shouts are joyful rather than aggressive.

## Visual Notes (for ComfyUI / Krea2 reference)

- **Present:** Harsh, dusty, warm-toned. The dirt pitch is brown and hard. The sky is a pale, washed-out blue. Zidhan's coaching stance is rigid. Jaisal is a still point in a moving scene.
- **The 'sweet' vision:** Golden, soft-edged, slightly unstable (heat-haze effect). The grass is lush. The light is warm and directional. Jaisal's rose-pink shirt is the focal point. The image flickers at the edges.
- **The 1950 sequence:** Even more golden. The field is expansive. The players are in period-appropriate kits. The light is a deep, late-afternoon gold. The edges dissolve into light.
