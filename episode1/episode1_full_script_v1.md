## 0. HOW TO READ THIS DRAFT

**Format decision: 20 + 20 (≈ 40 min).** I measured the dialogue before choosing:
- Your original Part 1 is ≈ 10–11 min on screen. With the missing setups added it fills ≈ 15 min, and ≈ 20 min with the optional ⊕ scenes.
- Your Part 2 brief needs ≈ 20 min at minimum. Every beat you asked for (letter, village, Sulaiman, recruitment, ship, Madras fire, tactics, anthem, five wins, the quarrel, the Uruguay loss, the handshake, the flip, the watch) is a scene, and a 15-minute Part 2 would have to drop most of them. A 15 + 15 version is listed in Section 4 with exactly what it loses.

**Legend**
- **[CORE]** is the locked-structure version. Part 1 CORE ≈ 15:00, Part 2 CORE ≈ 20:30.
- **[⊕ EXT]** optional scene. Part 1 ⊕ adds ≈ 5:00 to reach 20:00. Part 2 ⊕ adds ≈ 6:00 if you want fuller recruitment and the missing 1950 texture.
- **[NEW]** a line or beat I added to your original Part 1 text so Part 2 can pay it off. Everything not tagged [NEW] is your original wording.
- **⏱** is the estimated screen time. The timing method and the full table are in Section 3.

**Pace rule I used:** ≈ 130 spoken words per minute (Malayalam-English conversation with pauses). Montages and match sequences carry no dialogue budget; they run on picture and sound.

## 1. VISUAL & SOUND BIBLE

**2026 (Kerala):** full natural colour, wet green, red mud, harsh noon white. Handheld camera, close and sweaty. Everything a little too crowded.

**1950 (the mind-trip):** high-contrast black & white, golden-hour bloom, vignette edges dissolving into light. Locked-off or slow-gliding camera. **Colour survives in only three things:** the two graphic T-shirts (they drain slowly over the voyage), **fire** (gold), and **the watch** (a faint gold glint always).

**Sound rules (whole film):** no score, no needle-drop. Only what exists in the world: the Azan, the watch tick, wind, surf, oars, rigging, bare feet, coconut thuds, crowd claps, voices. The anthem is sung, never played.

**Anachronism rule:** Jaisal and Zidhan keep their modern words ("fax", "press", "load balancing"). The 1950 villagers translate them into their own life-language. That translation is the film's humour and its philosophy.

**Faces echo:** the 1950 villagers are played by the same faces as the 2026 kids. Jaisal recruits, without realising it, the children he already coaches around. The 360° spin reveals it.

## Scene 4: The Conversation of Dreams  ⏱ 3:15  [CORE]

**(Visual: Zidhan arrives at Jaisal's house. He stops at the window, looking inside. He sees Grandpa in his stillness.)**

**Zidhan:** (To himself) "He's dreaming again..."

**(Zidhan hears a rhythmic scratching sound coming from behind the house. He walks toward the backyard and finds Jaisal crouching in a hole, digging a grave. Beside the hole lies a dead wild boar.)**

**Zidhan:** (Confused) "What are you doing down there, Jaisal? Why the grave?"
**Jaisal:** (Looking up) "This wild boar got caught in the cracker trap. The dogs kept digging it up, so I'm digging a bigger one this time."
**(Jaisal pauses, looking at Zidhan with a philosophical glint.)**
**Jaisal:** "You know, Zidhan... dogs only know how to dig up the past things."
**(Zidhan laughs, realizing Jaisal is calling him a 'dog' for his obsession with previous failures.)**

**(NEW)** **Zidhan:** (The laugh fades) "You haven't been to the ground since the semi-final."
**(NEW — Jaisal doesn't answer. He digs. The scratch of the shovel is the only reply.)**

**Zidhan:** (Standing above him) "Fair enough. But listen, we need Adam for the final. Can you bring him?"
**Jaisal:** (Hesitating) "I don't know... maybe. Why the sudden rush? Is the team not ready?"
**Zidhan:** "The 'Rockstar Boys' are playing us. They are bigger and stronger. Tactics alone won't work; we need *Kaalappoott*. We need Adam's height to hold the line. Plus... if we win this, we can actually afford to keep the academy open. We might even buy a full bottle of first-aid spray!"

**(Jaisal looks at Zidhan's desperate face. He reaches his hand up from the grave.)**

**Jaisal:** "Alright... *nokkaam* (let's see). I'll try to bring him. Now, give me a hand to get out of here."
**(Zidhan pulls him up. Jaisal rises from the grave, a metaphor for returning to his passion.)**

**(Transition: Jaisal walks to the washbasin, washing his muddy hands and feet. As he grabs the towel, he looks over at Grandpa, who is now without his watch; it is resting on the table.)**

**Jaisal:** "You've been dreaming a lot lately, Grandpa."
**Grandpa:** (Softly) "Our dream is not to destroy others, Jaisalu... it is to live happily within them."
**Jaisal:** "What are you dreaming about now? My mother?"
**Grandpa:** "Many people dream about your mother. But right now, I am dreaming that no one in my family has ever left me. And I can see Subhash Chandra Bose... on the magnificent ship my father used to own."
**Jaisal:** "I think I'll try that too. A little time travel to escape this reality."

**(NEW — Jaisal turns to leave. Behind him, a soft scrape: Grandpa slides the watch across the table with two fingers, without looking at him. Jaisal stops. A long beat. He picks it up and buckles it onto his wrist. The tick is audible, then quiet.)**
