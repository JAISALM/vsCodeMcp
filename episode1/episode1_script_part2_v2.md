**The rules of the 1950 world**
1. **Palette:** high-contrast black & white, golden-hour bloom, soft dissolving vignette at the frame edges. Three things keep colour: Jaisal's and Zidhan's graphic T-shirts (the colour drains slowly over the voyage), **fire** (gold), and **the watch** (always faintly gold).
2. **Sound:** no score, no needle-drops. Only diegetic sound: the watch tick, wind, surf, oars, rigging, gulls, coconut thuds, bare feet on wood, crowd claps, human voices. Even the anthem is sung, not played.
3. **Faces echo:** the 1950 villagers are played by the same faces as the 2026 team and the people around it. Jaisal, without knowing it, recruits the kids he already coaches around. This pays off when the camera spins.
4. **Anachronism rule:** Jaisal and Zidhan keep their modern words ("fax", "load balancing", "press"). The locals translate everything into their own life-language. That translation is where the humour lives.
5. **Everything is Jaisal's imagination.** Logic bends where emotion needs it (the sea is shorter when you need it). But the football rules of 1950 stay real: heavy wet ball, rough tackling, no substitutes, no VAR, gamesmanship.

# PHASE 1: THE AWAKENING

## Scene 7: The Silent Stadium

**Visual:** Close on the watch dial under Jaisal's thumb, turning one notch. The fight in the paddy field — the shouting, the thud of the ball — is sucked into a single sound: *tick.* The colour drains from the mud outwards, starting at the watch and spreading like ink in water. The light turns amber. The gallery benches are empty — they were always empty, wooden planks and dust. The 11-year-olds are gone. The mud keeps their footprints, glossy and black.

Jaisal stands alone in the gallery. Down at the pitch edge, Zidhan is frozen with his mouth open mid-shout, the **notepad and pen still in his hand.** He looks at his wristwatch. The digital screen is blank.

**Sound:** Silence. *Tick. Tick.* A distant coconut frond brushing wind.

**Jaisal:** (Shouting down, the echo carrying) "ZIDHAN!"

**(Zidhan turns slowly. Jaisal runs down the gallery steps, each step a hollow wooden knock. He reaches the pitch edge and grabs Zidhan by both shoulders.)**

**Jaisal:** "Don't panic."

**Zidhan:** "Why would I panic? The kids are gone. The referee's gone. My watch is dead. Why would I panic, Jaisal?"

**Jaisal:** "It's 1950."

**Zidhan:** (A long beat; he looks at the empty gallery, then at Jaisal) "We were standing in mud two minutes ago."

**Jaisal:** "We're still standing in mud. Look down."

**(Zidhan looks. The mud is the same mud, now black and white.)**

**Zidhan:** "Why are we here?"

**Jaisal:** (Frustration rising) "Look at what we left. A rat in the middle of the pitch. A first-aid spray that goes *pssst* and nothing. Eleven-year-olds who foul because they think fouling is football. That's not their fault, Zidhan. It's the system. The politicians who build stadiums for the photograph. The department that exists on paper. The clubs that die before the kids grow. Nobody nurtures anything here. If we change the history here, the future — our academy — will breathe."

**Zidhan:** (Quietly) "You've been saying 'the system' since the semi-final."

**Jaisal:** "Because it *is* the system."

**Zidhan:** "And the system is a place you stopped visiting."

**(Jaisal flinches, as if a stone hit him. He looks away at the horizon.)**

**Jaisal:** "India was invited to the 1950 World Cup in Brazil. It said no. We're going to say yes."

**Zidhan:** "We're two people."

**Jaisal:** "We aren't the players, Zidhan. We're the coaches. We're bringing the brains of 2026 to the heart of 1950."

**Zidhan:** (Dry) "I've got eleven kids, one notebook, and an empty spray. That's my brain of 2026."

## Scene 7B: The Notebook (Zidhan's Mastery)

**Visual:** Jaisal holds out his hand. Zidhan opens the notebook to find a blank page, and we see what he has been carrying:
- Pages of hand-drawn circles and arrows, drills for children with no cones: "stones, 6 m apart."
- A column headed **"Injury log"**: *Adil — ankle, March. Jishnu — wrist, May. Fahad — left knee, don't let him head the ball.*
- A page of numbers in a child's handwriting: *Spray: 1 bottle. Used: 3. Left: ?*
- A tiny drawing of a goal with a dead rat beside it and a question mark.

**(Jaisal slows. He turns two pages. He sees the work: this man knows the bones of every child on that pitch, and has been coaching with nothing for years. He closes the page gently, finds the first blank sheet, and tears it out carefully so as not to damage the others.)**

**Jaisal:** "Pen."

**Zidhan:** "I'm a coach. I always have a pen."

**(Jaisal writes on his knee. We see only the last line, in his handwriting, drawn large and clear: *"Our dream is not to destroy others. It is to live happily within them."* The rest is hidden by his hand. He folds it in four and gives it to Zidhan.)**

**Jaisal:** "Go to the shore. Find **Sulaiman.** My grandfather's father. Give him this."

**Zidhan:** "How will I know him?"

**Jaisal:** "He'll be standing where the sea is longest. He's the one who stops to look at the horizon when everyone else is looking at the market."

**Zidhan:** "And then?"

**Jaisal:** "He'll make the ship ready. We stop at Madras before we sail for Brazil. And Zidhan — there's something else."

**(Jaisal's face hardens.)**

**Jaisal:** "Somewhere in this country there's a message going out. A cable. It says India will not participate. It goes out of Madras. The Federation sends it, and a post office presses it through. That cable cannot reach FIFA."

**Zidhan:** "How do you stop a cable?"

**Jaisal:** "However you can."

**Zidhan:** "Burn the post office?"

**Jaisal:** "If you must."

**Zidhan:** (Deadpan, tucking the letter into his pocket) "I'll do it."

**Jaisal:** "Zidhan, I didn't mean—"

**Zidhan:** "You said *if I must.*"

**(Zidhan walks off. Jaisal watches him go, half-smiling, half-terrified.)**

## Scene 8: The Only Colour

**Visual:** The stadium gate opens onto a dirt road, wet from some earlier rain. Palm trunks, wooden fences, a tin-roofed tea shop. Everything is black-and-white and smells of woodsmoke. Jaisal and Zidhan walk down the road side by side. They are the only ones in colour: a faded teal and a mustard T-shirt, each with a **glossy printed graphic** that no one in this world has seen.

**Sound:** Chickens. A distant hammer. A bullock cart's creak. Their own footsteps.

**(The village notices, without hostility: that is the point. They are curious, soft, and respectful.)**
- A **boy** (6) steps in front of Jaisal and silently touches the printed logo with one finger. He looks at his finger as if it might have colour on it.
- Two **old women** at a well stop mid-pull and exchange a look. One nods at Jaisal as if he were a guest at a wedding.
- A **tailor** at his sewing machine looks up. His eyes trace the T-shirt's seams, counting stitches. He stands and quietly steps outside to look closer, still holding a half-sewn shirt.
- A **toddy-shop owner** lifts a glass to Zidhan as he passes: *"cheers,"* with no language.
- A **constable** (we'll remember him) tips his cap to them in respect, then frowns at their sandals, then shrugs.
- A **man on a bicycle** slows, swivels his head to watch them go, rides straight into a fence post, picks himself up, and nods in apology to the post.

**Zidhan:** (Whispering) "They're staring at us."

**Jaisal:** "They're not staring. They're welcoming."

**Zidhan:** "Why are they smiling?"

**Jaisal:** "Because we look like someone's idea of tomorrow."

**(A pair of kids start trailing them, mimicking Zidhan's pocketed-hand walk. Zidhan stops. They stop. He starts. They start. He grins. This is the first time we see him smile in 1950.)**

**(At a fork in the road, one path leads toward the coconut groves and the market, the other toward the shining stripe of the sea. They stop.)**

**Zidhan:** "Sea?"

**Jaisal:** "Sea. I'll find the team."

**Zidhan:** "Which team?"

**Jaisal:** "That's the problem."

**(They part. We stay a moment on Zidhan's back, the letter bulging in his pocket, as he walks toward the light.)**

## Scene 9: The Man Who Stands Where the Sea Is Longest

**Visual:** A wide shore, white sand turned silver by the black-and-white. Fishing boats pulled up on the beach; nets drying on poles like giant cobwebs. Far out, anchored, rises the **MARJAN**: a towering wooden uru, three decks high, painted eyes on the bow looking towards the horizon.

On the shore, a tall, broad man stands alone with his hands behind his back, looking at the sea as if he were reading a letter from it. **Sulaiman.** He is in his fifties, white-streaked beard, a plain lungi, a dark waistcoat, a thick leather belt. **There is no watch on his wrist.**

**Sound:** Surf. Gulls. Zidhan's feet on wet sand.

**(Zidhan walks up and stops a respectful distance away. Sulaiman does not turn.)**

**Sulaiman:** (Still facing the sea) "Tell me before you say anything. Are you carrying good news or bad?"

**Zidhan:** "I don't know. I'm carrying a letter."

**(Sulaiman turns. His eyes go to Zidhan's T-shirt, then to his face. He is not afraid. He waits.)**

**Zidhan:** (Handing over the folded page) "From someone who says he's family."

**(Sulaiman unfolds the page. He reads slowly. We watch only his face. His eyes travel down the page, reach the last line, stop. His lips move silently, shaping the words. He looks up at the horizon, then back down at the page.)**

**Sulaiman:** (Quietly) "Where did he get those words?"

**Zidhan:** "From his grandfather."

**Sulaiman:** "My son is five years old. I only say those words to him at night, and only when he is asleep."

**(A long silence. The tide laps. Sulaiman folds the letter and puts it in his waistcoat, next to his heart.)**

**Sulaiman:** "Whose are you?"

**Zidhan:** "Nobody's. I'm a coach."

**Sulaiman:** "Coach?"

**Zidhan:** "I stand at the edge and send other people in."

**(Sulaiman considers this. A slow smile.)**

**Sulaiman:** "Then you'll do well at sea." (Turning toward the village, shouting) "MUALLIM!"

**(An old man in a skull-cap, a rolled chart under his arm, scrambles down the beach from a boat. Muallim Moideen. His eyes are milky, but he points without hesitation at the horizon, reading the wind.)**

**Sulaiman:** "The Madras road. How long?"

**Muallim:** "By the *Marjan*? Eight days. By the pathemari, with this wind? Three."

**Sulaiman:** "Give him the pathemari."

**Muallim:** "The pathemari doesn't carry strangers."

**Sulaiman:** "He isn't a stranger. He's carrying my letter."

**(Muallim looks at Zidhan with milky eyes. A long pause. He nods.)**

**Muallim:** (To Zidhan) "Can you swim?"

**Zidhan:** "Not very well."

**Muallim:** "Good. Then you'll hold on."

**(A slim, fast, lateen-sailed boat, the pathemari, rocks at the water's edge. Zidhan climbs aboard. The crew pushes off. The triangular sail fills. Zidhan sits on the deck planks, clutching his notebook. He looks back once at Sulaiman, who is already looking at the horizon again.)**

**Zidhan:** (To himself, nervous) "Three days..."

**(CUT TO: the pathemari, a speck at the horizon. The sun sets and rises twice in a single dissolve. Zidhan, asleep against a coil of rope, wakes to the smell of a new city. The sea is shorter when you need it.)**

# PHASE 2: THE LIFE-IQ SELECTION

## Scene 10: The Strong He Walks Past

**Visual:** Jaisal alone, walking through the village towards the coconut groves. He talks to himself, practising his own rules like a man memorising a speech.

**Jaisal:** (Muttering) "Not the strong. Not the fast. Patterns. Football is just life with a ball. Find the life."

**(He passes, in quick succession, three men that any normal scout would pick.)**
- **The bull-handlers:** two massive men holding a pair of festival bulls (Kaalappoott) by the rope, muscles like trunks. A beat as Jaisal walks past. One of the men flexes at him, hopefully. Jaisal pats the bull politely and keeps walking. *(Foreshadow: Zidhan's Part 1 word: Kaalappoott.)*
- **A porter** carrying an impossible stack of rice sacks on his head. Jaisal tips an imaginary hat and keeps walking.
- **A kalari fighter** at the edge of a mud pit, mid-demonstration: a flying kick, silhouetted against the sun. A small crowd claps. Jaisal watches for half a second, shakes his head with a smile. "Too much *push.*"

**(The kalari man sees Jaisal reject him and frowns, offended.)**

**(We hold on Jaisal's confident back as he walks on. In a later phase, we'll remember this.)**

## Scene 11: The Coconut Calculator

**Visual:** A grove: coconut palms leaning in every direction, trunks like grey columns, fronds slashing the sun into bars. Under one palm stands a thin man in wire spectacles and an immaculate folded mundu, looking up at the crown with his lips moving silently. **Gopalan.** He is counting.

**Jaisal:** (Approaching) "Excuse me. How many coconuts on that branch?"

**(Gopalan doesn't look away from the tree.)**

**Gopalan:** "Twelve."

**(Jaisal glances up, begins counting under his breath, "one, two, three...", gets lost around seven, tries again, gives up. Gopalan watches him, politely.)**

**Jaisal:** "I'll trust you. If I throw one stone at the middle, how many fall?"

**Gopalan:** (A brief glance) "Seven."

**Jaisal:** "Why seven?"

**Gopalan:** "The third from the left is already half-detached. The fifth is tied to the sixth by a twist of fibre. The ninth is heavy, it'll go when the branch shakes. If you hit the middle..." (He counts on his fingers.) "...seven."

**(Jaisal picks up a stone and throws. We follow it in slow motion. It hits the branch. Coconuts fall: *thud, thud, thud, thud, thud, thud, thud*. Jaisal counts. Seven. A beat. Then, after a long moment, an eighth coconut drops with a lazy *thud*.)**

**Jaisal:** "Eight."

**Gopalan:** (Without a trace of concern) "That one would have fallen by evening. It doesn't count."

**(Jaisal stares at him, delighted.)**

**Jaisal:** "What do you do?"

**Gopalan:** "I'm a coconut counter."

**Jaisal:** "What's that?"

**Gopalan:** "Merchants hire me to tell them what a grove will yield before the harvest. I'm never wrong. They don't pay me enough."

**Jaisal:** "How would you like to see the pass before the ball is kicked?"

**Gopalan:** (Adjusting spectacles) "I don't understand."

**Jaisal:** "Nobody does at first. Midfielder."

**Gopalan:** "What's a midfielder?"

**Jaisal:** "The man who counts what everyone else is guessing."

**(Gopalan thinks. He folds the end of his mundu tighter, which is a sign.)**

**Gopalan:** "Does it pay more?"

**Jaisal:** "It pays in World Cups."

**Gopalan:** "I don't know what that is."

**Jaisal:** "Neither do I, yet. Come."

**(Gopalan falls into step behind him, still counting the coconuts that remain, softly.)**

## Scene 12: The Wave-Watcher

**Visual:** The shore, an hour later. A fisherman stands waist-deep in the shallows, absolutely still. **Antappan.** His eyes are half-closed against the glare. A short spear hangs loose in his right hand.

**(Jaisal and Gopalan watch from the beach. Nothing happens for a very long time. The tide moves. A gull lands on a pole and leaves. Gopalan checks an imaginary watch.)**

**(Suddenly Antappan's arm flicks. The spear leaves his hand, skipping across the surface and plunging. In the same motion he reaches down and catches a leaping fish, bare-handed, mid-air, and holds it up, still thrashing.)**

**(He releases it, back into the water. He doesn't smile. He steps back one pace to a higher rock a moment before the next wave arrives.)**

**Jaisal:** (Walking up) "How did you know about the wave?"

**Antappan:** "I didn't."

**Jaisal:** "You stepped back before it came."

**Antappan:** "I stepped back because the sea changed its breath."

**Jaisal:** (Picking up a coconut husk from the sand, throwing it hard at Antappan's face without warning) "Catch!"

**(Antappan's hand intercepts it before it reaches his chin, without looking. He lowers it slowly and finally looks at Jaisal for the first time.)**

**Antappan:** "You shouldn't throw things at a man who's working."

**Jaisal:** "How do you do it? You don't even look at the fish."

**Antappan:** "I don't look at the fish. I look at where it'll be afraid."

**(Jaisal freezes. He turns to Gopalan.)**

**Jaisal:** (Whispering) "That's the best thing anyone's ever said about goalkeeping."

**Gopalan:** "What's a goalkeeper?"

**Jaisal:** "A man who looks at where the striker will be afraid."

**Antappan:** (Calmly) "I've never been afraid."

**Jaisal:** "That's why you'll be good at it."

## Scene 13: The Market (The Man Who Says No)

**Visual:** A chaotic vegetable market at midday. Baskets of chilli and gourd and banana, shouting vendors, bullock carts, a goat that nobody owns, a hundred bodies in constant motion. In the middle of it all stands a tall, thin man in a tilted cap, conducting the chaos with two fingers. **Moosa.** He nudges a child from a cart's path, tilts a basket to avoid a collision, stops an old woman from stepping back into a puddle. He never raises his voice.

**(A porter's basket tips. Moosa catches it with two fingers and slides it back into the porter's hands, never breaking eye contact with a customer he's bargaining with.)**

**Jaisal:** (Under his breath, to Gopalan and Antappan, who've followed) "Look at him. Nobody collides. Nobody stops. He's not leading. He's making room."

**Gopalan:** "He's taking the weight off the busiest corners."

**Jaisal:** "Say that again."

**Gopalan:** "He's balancing the load."

**(Jaisal's eyes light up. He crosses to Moosa.)**

**Jaisal:** "Sir, would you like to stop being a market manager?"

**Moosa:** (Not looking at him, nudging a boy aside) "Who'd stop the market?"

**Jaisal:** "It stops by itself. It always has."

**Moosa:** (Finally looking) "Then what's the job?"

**Jaisal:** "Defender."

**Moosa:** "From what?"

**Jaisal:** "From people who want to get through."

**Moosa:** "Everyone wants to get through."

**Jaisal:** "Exactly."

**(Moosa pauses. Sizes Jaisal up. And, casually, with two fingers, tilts Jaisal's shoulder out of the path of a passing donkey. Jaisal didn't see it coming. Moosa nods.)**

**Moosa:** "I'll come. But I need a partner. I don't like being the only one who says no."

**(At that exact moment, a short distance away, we hear a very loud, very bored voice:)**

**Pakru:** "No."

**(A heavy-browed man sits on an upturned crate with folded arms. He is being asked by a merchant to move his crate. He says "No." The merchant leaves. A second man asks. "No." A third. "No." A boy asks if he can have a banana. "No." He's holding a banana.)**

**Moosa:** (Quietly, to Jaisal) "That's Pakru. They call him Illa. He's said no to everything since he was nine."

**(Jaisal approaches Pakru. Gopalan and Antappan watch, curious.)**

**Jaisal:** "Will you play football?"

**Pakru:** "No."

**Jaisal:** "Will you let anyone score?"

**Pakru:** "No."

**Jaisal:** "Will you let anyone through?"

**Pakru:** "No."

**Jaisal:** "Will you stand in front of someone who's trying to run past you for ninety minutes and refuse them?"

**Pakru:** (A long pause. The faintest twitch of one eyebrow.) "...No."

**Jaisal:** (Turning triumphantly to the others) "Perfect."

**Gopalan:** "He said no."

**Jaisal:** "That's the best defender I've ever met."

**(Pakru stands for the first time, unfolds his arms, and falls into line behind the group without a word. Moosa and Pakru exchange a look. They have clearly been waiting for this.)**

## Scene 14: The Tea Shop (The Long Pass and the Eyebrow)

**Visual:** A crowded roadside tea shop. Benches, a huge brass samovar, a wall of steaming glasses. Behind the counter stands **Bappu**, a lean man with a tea-cloth over his shoulder. He pulls tea in long arcs between two steel glasses, a ribbon of liquid held a metre in the air, never spilling a drop.

**Jaisal:** (Watching) "Could you pass that to me?"

**(Bappu looks at him, looks at the glass in his hand, and, with a lazy flick of the wrist, sends the tea in a long arc across the shop. Jaisal catches the glass, full. Not a drop on the floor. The whole tea shop applauds. Jaisal looks at the glass, then at Bappu, awed.)**

**Jaisal:** (To the team, with a hushed voice) "A long pass. Over a table. With the weight just right."

**Bappu:** "It's tea."

**Jaisal:** "It's a pass."

**(In a corner of the tea shop, four men play the card game Twenty-Eight. One of them, **Thomas**, hooded eyes, ink-stained thumb, one eyebrow higher than the other, is losing nothing.)**

**Jaisal:** (Sitting down, deliberately) "Deal me in."

**(Thomas deals. Jaisal plays a hand. And loses. Thomas puts the cards face down.)**

**Thomas:** "You have two kings and a nine. You're hoping I won't lead hearts."

**Jaisal:** "...How did you..."

**Thomas:** "You play with your left eyebrow."

**(Jaisal reaches up to check his eyebrow. The others laugh.)**

**Jaisal:** "What's the job?"

**Thomas:** "I'm a card player."

**Jaisal:** "I mean: what do you *do*?"

**Thomas:** "I watch what people do before they do it. They always tell you with some part of their face."

**Jaisal:** "Do you know the word 'press'?"

**Thomas:** "Like tea?"

**Jaisal:** "Like a trap. You read the tell. You jump on the man who has the ball. He looks down at his foot: that's his tell. You close him."

**Thomas:** (Rising slowly) "He looks at his foot."

**Jaisal:** "Always."

**Thomas:** "I want to see that."

**(Thomas picks up his cards, tucks them in his waistband, and follows Jaisal out. Bappu removes his apron. The shop owner shouts after him. Bappu keeps pouring tea in the air as he walks out, arcing it to a small child who catches the glass and drinks.)**

## Scene 15: The Backwaters and the Palm Tops (The Wings)

**Visual:** A river flowing through green paddy: a long slim boat, a *vallam*, slices through the water, twelve rowers in perfect unison. At the front a stroke-caller sets the rhythm with a single clap. **Kuttappan**, broad-shouldered, calloused hands, glides his oar like a part of his own arm.

**Sound:** *Creak, dip, pull. Creak, dip, pull.* The clap. The water.

**(Jaisal on the bank watches the oars. He grins. He calls to Kuttappan, who rows to the bank and looks at him curiously.)**

**Jaisal:** "Can you change sides mid-stroke without anyone noticing?"

**Kuttappan:** "I do it every race."

**Jaisal:** "That's what I need on the wing."

**(On the far bank, from the top of a palm tree thirty feet up, a thin voice calls down.)**

**Karunan:** "Is he buying?"

**(A wiry man descends a coconut palm headfirst, with a rope looped around his ankles, bare feet gripping the bark like claws, knife in his teeth, a clay pot of toddy tied to his belt. He lands on the ground in one jump. Karunan. A knife-thin grin.)**

**Jaisal:** "Can you jump from that tree to that one?"

**(Karunan looks at the next palm, six feet away.)**

**Karunan:** "Why?"

**Jaisal:** "I want to see."

**(Karunan climbs. He swings his rope loop, jumps, catches the next trunk, and slides down.)**

**Jaisal:** "Left wing."

**Karunan:** "What's left wing?"

**Jaisal:** "Wherever the defender isn't looking."

**Karunan:** (Delighted) "I've been doing that all my life."

## Scene 16: The Husk Beaters (The Engine Room)

**Visual:** A beach strewn with coconut husks. A dozen men sit in rows, beating husks against the ground with wooden mallets and then with their own feet, thick calves pounding the fibre, rhythmically, the sound a drumbeat: *thump, thump, thump.* The air is full of brown dust. Ropes spool out of the husk piles.

**(Two of them are enormous: **Chandu**, wide jaw, thick neck, calves like trunks; and **Velayudhan**, short, barrel-chested, shaved and sunburnt head, a gap in his teeth.)**

**Jaisal:** (To Chandu) "Kick that husk."

**(Chandu kicks. A husk leaves the beach, sails across the palm tops, and vanishes.)**

**Jaisal:** "Can you kick it softly?"

**Chandu:** (Honestly baffled) "What's soft?"

**Velayudhan:** (Laughing) "He's never done it."

**Jaisal:** "That's fine. We'll teach you. First, I need to know: can you run for ninety minutes?"

**Chandu:** "I've beaten husks since sunrise. It's noon."

**Jaisal:** (To Gopalan, quietly) "Fullbacks."

**(Chandu and Velayudhan exchange a long look and silently put down their mallets.)**

## Scene 17: The Healer (The Callback)

**Visual:** A small hut near the shore, hung with bundles of dried herbs. Inside, an elderly man, **Vaidyar Madhavan**, kneels over a patient, working warm coconut oil into a swollen ankle with slow, circular thumbs. A boy sits across from him, wincing.

**(Jaisal stands at the door, holding his breath. He watches the swelling visibly go down in a single shot.)**

**Jaisal:** (Softly, almost to himself) "Pssssst. Nothing."

**(He steps in. Vaidyar looks up without surprise.)**

**Jaisal:** "I'm not here for the legs that run. I'm here for the legs that break."

**Vaidyar:** (Grunting) "That's every leg."

**Jaisal:** "I know. I need a third coach."

**Vaidyar:** "I'm a healer."

**Jaisal:** "That's the coach."

**(Vaidyar wipes his hands. He packs a small cloth bag with bottles of oil, rolled bandages, a stone mortar.)**

## Scene 18: The Thief (The Last Piece)

**Visual:** A market alley, two hours later. Jaisal, Gopalan, Antappan, Moosa, Pakru, Thomas, Bappu, Kuttappan, Karunan, Chandu, Velayudhan, and Vaidyar are walking together in a ragged line, looking like a very confused wedding procession. Gopalan is counting something. Moosa is conducting the traffic around them with two fingers.

**(Suddenly, a shout behind them: "STOP! THIEF!")**

**(A lean man in a torn shirt comes tearing around the corner, two constables behind him. **Raghavan.** Scar through his left eyebrow, toes gripping the cobbles.)**

**(He sprints through the market: he pivots left without slowing, leaps over a basket of fish, slides under a wooden table, comes up on the other side without breaking stride, slaloms between two cattle, feints right at the constables, and goes left. The constables slam into each other. Raghavan doesn't look back.)**

**(Jaisal watches, jaw slack.)**

**Jaisal:** (Almost reverent) "Look at his feet."

**Gopalan:** "He's changing direction every second."

**Antappan:** "He doesn't look at where he's going. He looks at where they aren't."

**Jaisal:** "Wait here." (He runs.)

**(Jaisal chases the thief through the market. He's clumsy, 2026 trainers on 1950 cobbles. He knocks over a fishwife's basket. She screams. He grabs the basket, tries to fix it, a dozen mackerel slither across the street. He can't leave. He picks up the basket, tucks it under one arm, and keeps running with it.)**

**Jaisal:** (Shouting, out of breath) "Wait! Stop!"

**(Raghavan stops abruptly at a dead end, back to a wall, panting. He holds up both hands.)**

**Raghavan:** "Are you police too?!"

**Jaisal:** (Wheezing, a basket of fish still under his arm) "Nope."

**Raghavan:** "Then why are you chasing me?"

**Jaisal:** "Because you have the same feet as a champion."

**Raghavan:** (Staring at the basket) "Why are you carrying fish?"

**Jaisal:** "It's a long story."

**(Behind them, the two constables arrive, panting. The one who tipped his cap to Jaisal earlier sees him, and stops. He sees the basket of fish. He sees the thief. He decides.)**

**Constable:** (To the other, loudly) "He's not the one."

**(They walk away. Raghavan watches them go, stunned.)**

**Raghavan:** "What do you want from me?"

**Jaisal:** "A forward."

**Raghavan:** "What's a forward?"

**Jaisal:** "A man who runs towards the thing everyone else is running from."

**(A beat. Raghavan almost smiles.)**

**Raghavan:** "I'm very good at that."

**(CUT TO BLACK. Sound: the creak of a ship.)**

## Scene 19: The Ship — "Vaappaa!"

**Visual:** The shore at dusk. The **MARJAN** sits at anchor, now brought in close. A long gangplank. The team is lined up on the beach: eleven men and a healer, looking at the ship. At the centre of the line stands Raghavan, the thief, still holding the basket of fish, a faint smell around him. They look like a village photograph.

**(Sulaiman stands at the foot of the gangplank, hands behind his back. Jaisal walks up to him. They face each other. Sulaiman looks at him for a long moment, then, slowly, at Jaisal's wrist.)**

**(On Jaisal's wrist: the watch. A faint gold glow in the monochrome. Sulaiman's hand rises, slowly, and touches the watch face. His fingers rest on the glass. We hear the *tick.* He looks at Jaisal's face.)**

**Sulaiman:** (Quietly) "That clock keeps strange time."

**Jaisal:** (Voice breaking) "It's very old."

**Sulaiman:** "It will find its way to my son."

**(Jaisal can only nod. He is fighting tears. He cannot say anything, and the audience knows why.)**

**(And then: a small voice, running along the sand.)**

**Little Abu Bakar:** "VAAPPAAAA!"

**(A five-year-old boy in a tiny white mundu runs full tilt down the beach. He leaps. Sulaiman catches him mid-air and swings him up onto one arm in a single easy motion. The boy is giggling. He wraps his arms round his father's neck.)**

**(Jaisal's whole face changes. He is looking at his grandfather, the old man in a quiet chair, the hand he'd place tea in, as a five-year-old boy.)**

**(The camera pushes in on Jaisal's eyes. Then on the child's. The child turns his head. He is looking at Jaisal.)**

**Little Abu Bakar:** "What's your name?"

**Jaisal:** (Almost a whisper) "Jaisal."

**Little Abu Bakar:** (Trying out the word, with a small head tilt) "Jaisalu?"

**(Jaisal laughs, and a tear runs down his face. It's the exact tone his grandfather uses in 2026. He doesn't wipe the tear.)**

**Jaisal:** "Yes. Jaisalu."

**Sulaiman:** (Gently, setting the boy down) "He asks everyone's name."

**(He ruffles the child's hair. The boy runs up the gangplank. The team begins to follow him, one by one. Raghavan is last, still holding the fish.)**

**Sulaiman:** (To Jaisal, quietly) "I don't know who you are. But I know whose words you carry. The sea is open."

**(Jaisal nods. He looks up at the ship's painted eyes. They look back.)**

# PHASE 3: THE MADRAS FIRE & THE VOYAGE

## Scene 20: Casting Off — The Coconut Ball

**Visual:** Dawn. The **MARJAN** slides out of the shallows, her huge square sail unfurling with a sound like a wing. The village shrinks on the beach: the tailor, the kalari man, the little boy who touched the T-shirt, all waving. Jaisal stands on the stern deck, watching Kerala become a green line.

**Sound:** Rigging creak. Wind in canvas. The slow slap of the hull. Gulls fade.

**(On the main deck, the team stands in an awkward circle, hands at their sides, like schoolboys on the first day. Jaisal walks into the circle and drops a coconut at his feet.)**

**Jaisal:** "This is a football."

**Gopalan:** (Gently) "That is a coconut."

**Jaisal:** "In the place we're going, it's the same thing."

**(He kicks it. It bounces off the deck planks in a wild, unpredictable direction, hits a rope coil, and rebounds into Velayudhan's stomach. Velayudhan folds. The team laughs for the first time.)**

**Jaisal:** "The ball in Brazil will be heavy leather. When it's wet it'll be heavier. It won't bounce true on their pitches. You'll never get a perfect touch. So we start with a bad one. If you can control a coconut on a rolling ship, you can control anything on land."

**(Karunan takes the first run at it. The coconut skews off his toes into the sea. Splash. Everyone looks over the rail. A fish leaps out with the coconut on its nose, or so it seems. Antappan, without a word, reaches over the rail and pulls the coconut back out of the water by hand. He tosses it gently back to Jaisal.)**

**Antappan:** "It floats."

**Jaisal:** "That's the first thing anyone's learned today."

**Visual (Montage, 40 seconds):** Players pass coconuts across the deck. Thomas learns to watch the shoulders, not the feet. Bappu's long passes arc over the mast, landing in a bucket. Pakru refuses to let any coconut past him: he kicks, heads, and finally just sits on it. Chandu learns what "soft" means: his first gentle pass lands in Moosa's hands, and Chandu stares at his foot in astonishment. Raghavan feints past three men, steals the coconut from Gopalan, and politely hands it back.

**Sound:** Bare feet on planks. Thuds. Laughter. The sea.

**(Jaisal looks at Raghavan, who is juggling the coconut on his instep with a bored expression. Jaisal smiles. The shot lingers on Jaisal's face, his eyes on a player, as if looking at a painting.)**

**(MATCH CUT: The coconut, spinning in the air, becomes a street-lamp globe, swinging in a harbour at night.)**

## Scene 21: The Madras Post Office (The Butterfly Effect)

**Visual:** Madras, dawn. Zidhan steps off the pathemari onto a stone jetty, squinting. A big, noisy city: trams, rickshaws, bullock carts, newspaper boys, the smell of fish and coal smoke. A sea of white dhotis and black umbrellas. He asks directions with a notebook in hand. A rickshaw puller points. Zidhan follows.

**(The Post Office: a tall, red-brick building, arched windows, a long queue snaking up the stairs. Inside it's all brass counters, ledgers, rubber stamps, and ceiling fans turning slowly in the heat.)**

**Sound:** Fans. Stamps. Typewriters. A hundred murmured conversations.

**(Zidhan joins the queue. A clerk at the front takes a token. A sign on the wall: *"Telegrams: Counter 4."* He reaches Counter 4. Behind it, a stout postmaster with handlebar moustache, spectacles on a string, glares at a long strip of paper on the desk.)**

**(Zidhan reads the paper upside down. It reads, in neat capitals: *"INDIA WILL NOT PARTICIPATE IN FIFA WORLD CUP. LETTER FOLLOWS."* Beneath it, a signature: a federation secretary's.)**

**(The postmaster's finger hovers above a brass telegraph key.)**

**Zidhan:** (Politely, in his best formal English) "Sir, may I ask you to hold that cable?"

**Postmaster:** "Hold it? It's from the Federation. Paid in advance. Eleven words."

**Zidhan:** "I understand. But there may be a mistake."

**Postmaster:** "Mistakes go through the Federation. Not through Counter 4."

**Zidhan:** "May I speak with the Federation?"

**Postmaster:** "You need Form 7B."

**Zidhan:** "Where is Form 7B?"

**Postmaster:** "At Counter 11. But Counter 11 is closed until Thursday."

**Zidhan:** "What day is it?"

**Postmaster:** "Tuesday."

**(Zidhan closes his eyes, a man who has dealt with bureaucracy his whole life, in two eras.)**

**Zidhan:** (Quietly, to himself, a coach who has spent years begging for a bottle of spray) "I know this. I know this exact conversation."

**(Behind the postmaster, on a high shelf, a small shrine: a framed picture of Lord Ganesha, a garland of dried flowers, and a **brass lamp**, a lit oil lamp, small flame flickering. The postmaster lowers his hand towards the key.)**

**(Zidhan, desperate, steps back from the counter. His elbow, accidentally, knocks into a stack of ledgers. They tumble. The postmaster whips around. In the chaos, the stack of ledgers slides along the shelf, nudges the brass lamp. It tips.)**

**(Slow motion. The lamp turns in the air. Oil arcs out in a thin ribbon. It lands on the cable strip.)**

**(WHOOSH. Fire.)**

**Visual:** **A gout of golden flame** erupts across the desk: the only true colour in the monochrome room. It eats the cable strip, curls the paper, licks up the brass key. The room gasps. People scream. A clerk drops a tray of stamps.

**Postmaster:** (Frozen, then screaming) "SOMEONE! WATER!"

**(Zidhan grabs a wet sacking from a bucket by the door and throws it across the desk. The fire smothers. Smoke fills the counter. The cable is a blackened ribbon. The postmaster stares at it, grey with horror.)**

**Postmaster:** (Whispering) "The Federation paid in advance. They'll hang me."

**Zidhan:** (Quietly, at his shoulder) "Sir. The cable is gone. But they'll want something sent."

**Postmaster:** "...Something sent."

**Zidhan:** (Pulling a fresh form from the counter, writing quickly in the postmaster's own pen) "Short. Efficient. Impossible to misunderstand."

**(He slides the form across. In Zidhan's handwriting: *"INDIA IS COMING."* The postmaster, still stunned, reads it. He nods mechanically. He picks up the scorched key, taps. It sputters. A thin line of smoke curls from the machine. It taps out three letters. Four. Then a cracking sound and the key goes dead.)**

**Postmaster:** (Staring at the key) "It's sent. I think it's sent. Part of it is sent."

**Zidhan:** "Which part?"

**Postmaster:** "...India. And a little more."

**(Zidhan doesn't wait. He backs away from the counter, collects his notebook, and walks out of the post office as calmly as a man can walk out of a fire he caused. Behind him, a bell begins to clang: a fire cart approaching. He turns the corner and begins to run.)**

**Sound:** The distant bell. His own breath. Then the sea.

**(CUT TO: a telegraph office in Rio. A bored clerk cuts a half-garbled strip: *"INDIA ...C..."* He frowns, turns it over, shrugs, and drops it in a tray marked "Unresolved." No one will ever know whether it's yes or no. We will remember this in Brazil.)**

## Scene 22: The Market, the Socks, and the Question

**Visual:** A busy Madras bazaar, ten minutes later. Zidhan walks through it, breathing hard, pulling Sulaiman's coin pouch from his belt. He's now a shopper on a mission.
- A **seed vendor**: a mound of shining brown rubber seeds. Zidhan picks up a handful, rolls them in his palm: round, smooth, the size of a thumb joint. "Markers," he mutters. "Positions." He buys a bag.
- A **stationery stall**: stacks of paper. He buys a ream.
- A **sock seller** with a barrow piled with striped woollen socks. Zidhan buys fifteen pairs, bargaining in two sentences. The seller counts the coins twice. "For whom?" "For people who don't know they need them yet."

**(He loads the bag on his shoulder and walks to the harbour, where a large wooden shape is just anchoring: the MARJAN, her painted eyes looking at him from the bow.)**

**(On deck, the whole team is lined up at the rail, looking down at him. Jaisal stands at the front, grinning. He hasn't seen Zidhan for eight days.)**

**Zidhan:** (Climbing the gangplank, out of breath) "I did it."

**Jaisal:** "Did you?"

**Zidhan:** "I burned the post office."

**Jaisal:** (Horrified) "You *what*?"

**Zidhan:** "A little. It was an accident. A lamp."

**Jaisal:** "A lamp."

**Zidhan:** "A very small lamp."

**Jaisal:** (Pinching his nose) "Did the cable go through?"

**Zidhan:** "Part of it. I think."

**(Zidhan reaches the deck. He looks along the line of the team: a fisherman, a market man in a tilted cap, a card player, a tea-maker, a toddy tapper, two husk-beaters, a coconut accountant, a man holding a basket of fish. A healer with a bag of oil. He stares.)**

**Zidhan:** (Slowly) "Where are the other players?"

**Jaisal:** "These are the players."

**Zidhan:** "These are the players of the *World Cup*?"

**Jaisal:** "These are the players of India."

**Zidhan:** (Looking at Thomas, who looks back with one eyebrow raised) "He's in his sixties."

**Thomas:** "Forty-three."

**Zidhan:** (Looking at Raghavan) "Is that fish?"

**Raghavan:** "It was supposed to be dinner."

**(Zidhan turns slowly to Jaisal.)**

**Zidhan:** "Jaisal."

**Jaisal:** "We're the coaches, Zidhan. We need a coaching team of at least three. We have two and a healer."

**Zidhan:** "Three coaches for eleven fishermen."

**Jaisal:** "For eleven *minds.*"

**(Zidhan looks at the healer, who nods gravely. He looks at the ship. At the sea. He opens the bag on his shoulder and tosses the first pair of socks to Raghavan. Raghavan catches it and looks at it as if it were a live creature.)**

**Zidhan:** "Put them on. We'll start with feet."

**Raghavan:** "What's wrong with feet?"

**Zidhan:** "They're about to meet Brazil."

## Scene 23: The Route, the Sea, and the Ninety Minutes

**Visual:** Night, the main cabin. A hand-painted map of the world on a table, the Muallim's chart. A kerosene lamp swings gently. Muallim Moideen sits cross-legged, milky eyes on the horizon. He draws the route in chalk as if it were a prayer: Madras → Colombo → across the Arabian Sea → Aden → the Red Sea → Suez → the Mediterranean → Gibraltar → the Atlantic → Rio. The line wobbles, then straightens.

**Muallim:** "Forty days. If the wind loves us. Sixty if it doesn't."

**Zidhan:** (Looking at the map, then at the calendar in his notebook) "The first match is on a date I can't change."

**Muallim:** (Without looking) "Then the wind will love you."

**(CUT TO: dawn on deck. Zidhan stands at the mast with his notebook, a stopwatch he improvised from a hanging weight, and his arms folded.)**

**Zidhan:** "There are no substitutes in 1950. Eleven men play ninety minutes. If you're tired, you play tired. If you're hurt, you play hurt. If you're dead, you stand there."

**Pakru:** "No."

**Zidhan:** "No dying?"

**Pakru:** "No standing."

**Zidhan:** "Good. Run."

**Visual (Montage: The Conditioning, the sea as a gym):**
- **Dawn laps** around the main deck, bare feet slapping wet wood, Zidhan counting with a stick.
- **Karunan climbs the mast** hand over hand and drops to the deck, then climbs again. Chandu tries and the mast groans. The sailors place bets.
- **Antappan hauls a fishing net** over the stern with Velayudhan, both pulling in rhythm. The net comes up full of fish. The cook is thrilled.
- **Kuttappan teaches the team to row** the ship's lifeboat in a tight circle. They go round and round, perfectly synchronised, then abruptly crash into the hull. Laughter.
- **Vaidyar Madhavan works coconut oil into aching calves** at sunset, one by one, a quiet line of men sitting on coils of rope, his thumbs kneading, a ritual of its own.
- **A sleeping row on the deck at night**, Raghavan snoring, Gopalan counting stars under his breath, Thomas dealing a solitary hand.
- **Gopalan stops at the rail and watches a school of flying fish.** He moves his lips. A beat. "Forty-one."

**Sound:** Feet. Rope. Surf. A gasp. A whistle. A joke in Malayalam.

**(Zidhan walks along the line of players at dusk, checking each one like a doctor. At the end he stops by Jaisal.)**

**Zidhan:** "They're ready for ninety minutes."

**Jaisal:** "For the *tactics*?"

**Zidhan:** "For the ninety. The tactics are your department."

## Scene 24: The Tactics Board (The Rubber Seeds)

**Visual:** Day. The main deck, a clean square of planks. Jaisal kneels and, with a piece of chalk, draws the outline of a football pitch. The team gathers round. He takes out the bag of rubber seeds. Eleven brown. Eleven dark. He lays them out on the chalk.

**Jaisal:** "This is us. This is them. Everyone here has a job. Nobody has a number."

**(He explains each idea in the language of the person he explains it to.)**

**To Antappan and Bappu, about the back-pass:**
**Jaisal:** "When you have the ball and nobody is open, you don't throw it away. You give it back to the keeper. He holds it. He looks. He sees where they're afraid. Then he throws it."

**Antappan:** "That is how a net is thrown. You pull it back to cast it wide."

**Jaisal:** "Yes. Exactly."

**Pakru:** "Is this cowardice?"

**Jaisal:** "In 1950? Yes."

**Pakru:** (Eyebrow twitch) "I like it."

**To Kuttappan and Karunan, about inverted wingers:**
**Jaisal:** "Don't stay on the touchline. Cut in. The fullback will follow you, or he won't. If he follows, there's a gap behind him. If he doesn't, you have the middle."

**Kuttappan:** "Changing sides mid-stroke."

**Jaisal:** "Yes."

**Karunan:** "And if both of us cut in?"

**Jaisal:** "Then the fullbacks go forward and take the lane you left."

**Chandu:** (Frowning at his own seed) "So I walk where the wing used to be."

**Jaisal:** "You walk where the *space* used to be."

**To Moosa and Gopalan, about load balancing:**
**Jaisal:** "Nobody carries the whole basket. When one man is pressed, two others take the weight. When he's free, they step back."

**Moosa:** "That's what I do in the market. I don't lead. I make room."

**Gopalan:** "Balance. Fifty-fifty becomes sixty-forty becomes forty-sixty."

**Jaisal:** "Say it again, slowly."

**Gopalan:** "Whichever side is heavy, we lean the other way."

**To Thomas, about the press:**
**Jaisal:** "Don't chase everyone. Wait. Watch their shoulders and their eyes. When the man with the ball looks at his own foot..."

**Thomas:** (Smiling) "...close him."

**Jaisal:** "All of you. Together. Before he looks back up."

**(He moves the rubber seeds along the chalk in a flowing pattern: a wave, a squeeze, a trap. The team watches in silence. Zidhan, at the back, stands with arms folded. Gradually he leans forward. He is no longer sceptical. He is learning.)**

**Zidhan:** (Quietly, to Jaisal) "And when they kick us from behind?"

**Jaisal:** "They won't need to. We won't be there."

**(Zidhan's eyes narrow. He glances at the rubber seeds. He is not entirely convinced. He says nothing. We file this away.)**

**Detail:** Over the montage, the colour in the T-shirts has been draining, a little more each day. By the end of the deck scene, Jaisal's teal looks grey and Zidhan's mustard looks brown. Neither of them notices.

## Scene 25: The Night Deck (Bose)

**Visual:** Night. The Atlantic. A million stars. The ship rolls gently. The team sleeps on the main deck, a row of bodies under blankets. On a coil of rope near the bow, **little Abu Bakar** is asleep with his head on a folded sail. Jaisal sits beside him, awake, watching.

**(Sulaiman appears. He wraps the boy in a blanket and tucks it around his small shoulders. He sits beside Jaisal. They watch the sea together.)**

**Sulaiman:** "He talks in his sleep."

**Jaisal:** "What does he say?"

**Sulaiman:** "Everyone's name. He wakes up and he forgets all of them."

**(Jaisal smiles. He looks down at the boy. In 2026 this boy is an old man who stares at nothing and dreams of this deck.)**

**Sulaiman:** "My father taught me: a ship needs many hands but one eye."

**Jaisal:** "Which one am I?"

**Sulaiman:** (A small smile) "That's for the sea to say."

**(Sulaiman rises and goes below. Jaisal sits alone with the sleeping child. He looks up. At the very bow of the ship, silhouetted against the stars, he sees a man.)**

**(A tall figure in a long dark coat, hands clasped behind his back, round-rimmed spectacles catching the starlight. A cap. He's looking at the horizon with a calm, intent expression, as if waiting for a shore that has not been built yet.)**

**(Jaisal doesn't move. He doesn't breathe. His lips shape one word without sound.)**

**(The figure turns his head, half a degree, toward Jaisal. A faint nod. Then he is gone, as if the ship had breathed him in. The deck is empty. The stars are the same stars. Only the sleeping child stirs and mutters one word: *"Netaji."*)**

**(Jaisal looks down at the boy, then up at the empty bow. He smiles slowly. This is the ship his grandfather dreams of. This is the dream.)**

# PHASE 4: THE WORLD CUP

## Scene 26: Arrival — Brazil

**Visual:** Dawn. Mist on the water. Out of it, slowly, rises Rio de Janeiro: the green granite of Sugarloaf, the white statue of the Christ with open arms above the clouds, a curve of golden beach, a harbour crowded with steamers. The MARJAN glides in among them, a wooden ghost between iron liners.

**Sound:** Foghorns. Gulls. A distant roar: a stadium's worth of voices, as if the city itself were breathing.

**(On deck, the team lines up at the rail. They stare at the city in silence. Karunan whispers something in Malayalam. Gopalan counts the steamers and stops at forty. Antappan looks at the Christ statue the way he looks at the sea.)**

**(They disembark in their sailcloth kit: shirts sewn by Sulaiman's sailmakers from white canvas, dyed in turmeric and indigo, hand-stitched numbers; some wear Zidhan's striped socks, some go barefoot, some wear borrowed leather boots tied with rope. They carry their own coconuts, in a net. They look like the poorest team in the world, because they are.)**

**(On the quay, a crowd of dockworkers, porters, and children watches them pass. A boy runs alongside, staring at their feet. A newsboy shouts a headline in Portuguese. A policeman crosses himself.)**

## Scene 27: The Announcement

**Visual:** A cavernous stadium office, FIFA's tournament desk. A long table, ashtrays, flags of thirteen nations on little stands. At the head of the table, an official in a pinstripe suit reads from a sheet. A bored clerk in the corner holds up a tray marked *"Unresolved."* In it: a scorched slip of paper, *"INDIA ...C..."*

**Official:** (In English, to a room of journalists) "Group Three: Sweden, Italy, Paraguay. And, according to the draw, India."

**(A murmur. The official lifts the slip with two fingers.)**

**Official:** "We have received no confirmation of India's participation. We have also received no notice of withdrawal. We have a garbled cable from Madras, which our telegraphers read as 'India...' and then something. It may be a yes. It may be a no. It may be a typographical error."

**Journalist:** "So is India coming?"

**Official:** "The seat is held. The seat is *always* held. If India is coming, India will come."

**(Outside the window, on the road below, a line of eleven barefoot and half-shod men in sailcloth shirts walks up the hill towards the stadium with a ship's company behind them. The official sees them. His pen stops.)**

**Official:** (To the clerk, slowly) "Move India's slip from *Unresolved* to *Arrived.*"

## Scene 28: The Anthem

**Visual:** The opening match of Group Three, a vast stadium under an overcast sky. Thirty thousand spectators, a blur of hats. The two teams line up in the tunnel: Sweden in yellow, India in sailcloth. Zidhan and Jaisal and Vaidyar stand at the edge of the pitch, in their fading shirts, with the rubber-seed board under Zidhan's arm.

**(A uniformed band stands ready. The announcer calls the anthems. The Swedish anthem plays; the crowd politely applauds. Then, to a hush, the announcer reads:)**

**Announcer:** "India."

**(The band leader looks at his sheet. He looks at the band. They shake their heads. They do not have the music. A beat. Silence. Thirty thousand people hold their breath. The Indian players look at each other.)**

**(From the end of the line, in a thin, cracked voice, Gopalan begins to sing. Then Antappan. Then Pakru, who says "No," and sings anyway. Then Raghavan, still in the socks. Then Kuttappan, Bappu, Karunan, Thomas, Moosa, Chandu, Velayudhan. Then the sailors on the touchline. Then Zidhan. Then Jaisal.)**

**Sound:** **The Indian national anthem, sung with no instruments: forty voices, uneven, a little flat, intensely human.** Wind. A flag, snapping on a pole.

**(The crowd, puzzled, quiets. A Brazilian in the stands takes off his hat. Then another. By the last line, the stadium is standing in silence. When the singing stops, there is a long hush, and then an enormous, spontaneous roar.)**

**Zidhan:** (Whispering to Jaisal, eyes wet) "A dream of a billion people."

**Jaisal:** "And we're the ones singing it."

*(Note: "Jana Gana Mana" was adopted as India's national anthem in January 1950, which makes this the first time it is sung at a World Cup. Worth verifying before you lock this line.)*

## Scene 29: The First Match — India vs Sweden (The Shock)

**Visual:** The whistle. Sweden, the reigning Olympic champions, tall, blond, powerful, kick off. They press at once. The heavy leather ball skids and thuds. India's first touches are poor. Within three minutes a Swedish forward flattens Moosa with a shoulder charge from behind. The referee waves play on. Moosa stands, unhurt, pale. Zidhan on the touchline looks at Jaisal. Jaisal doesn't look back.

**(The Swedes attack again. Karunan wins a loose ball, passes it infield to Thomas. Thomas takes a touch. Two Swedes close him down. He looks at his foot, then at Pakru. He passes the ball back.)**

**(Pakru stops it with his sole. Looks up. Passes it back to Antappan.)**

**(Antappan picks up the ball. The stadium goes quiet. Sweden stops. They've never seen this: a team passing back to the goalkeeper. A Swedish forward tilts his head. ## Scene 32: India vs Brazil (Samba vs Modernity)

## Scene 33: The Quarrel

**Visual:** Night. The deck of the MARJAN, moored at the quay. The deck is lit by a single lantern. The team is asleep below. On the quarterdeck, Zidhan stands with his notebook open, the injury list long. Jaisal leans on the rail, looking at the lights of Rio.

**Sound:** Water against the hull. A distant ship's horn.

**Zidhan:** "Uruguay are in three days. They're the champions of the world. They'll hit us from behind. They'll hold our shirts. They'll stand on toes. They'll waste time and the referee will let them."

**Jaisal:** "We know."

**Zidhan:** "We're going to need fouls to keep up."

**Jaisal:** (Slowly) "No."

**Zidhan:** "I'm not talking about hurting anyone. I'm talking about sending a message. The first minute. A hard tackle. So they know we're not afraid."

**Jaisal:** "We won against Brazil by playing better football than Brazil. We won against Sweden, Italy, and Paraguay without a single dirty tackle. The whole world has seen it."

**Zidhan:** "The whole world saw us win. Nobody saw Moosa's ribs."

**Jaisal:** "That's what I'm saying. We win by thinking, not by *kicking*."

**Zidhan:** "This morning, in the mud. A boy made a tactical foul. A yellow card. It saved a goal. You saw a fight. I saw a goal saved."

**Jaisal:** "It started a fight."

**Zidhan:** "It saved a *goal*, Jaisal. Nobody counts that."

**(Jaisal's jaw tightens.)**

**Jaisal:** "Physicality is for people who can't think."

**Zidhan:** "Brazil can think. Brazil can do both."

**Jaisal:** "Brazil is *Brazil.*"

**Zidhan:** (Quietly) "Uruguay can do both too."

**(A long silence. Jaisal looks away.)**

**Jaisal:** "I won't ask them to cheat."

**Zidhan:** "It's not cheating if everyone's doing it."

**Jaisal:** (Turning, finally angry) "Then I'm done. I'm leaving."

**(Zidhan doesn't move. He looks at Jaisal for a long moment. When he speaks, his voice is very soft.)**

**Zidhan:** "This isn't new to you."

**Jaisal:** "What?"

**Zidhan:** "Leaving at the semi-final."

**(Jaisal stops. The sentence lands. It's the thing neither of them has said aloud since.)**

**Zidhan:** "You left the academy after the semi-final. You took the tactics and left the mud. You said it was the system. It was the system. But you also left."

**Jaisal:** "That's not fair."

**Zidhan:** "Dogs dig up the past, right? Fine. I'm a dog. But you buried it so badly. You buried it the way you bury the boar, not deep enough."

**(Jaisal can't answer. He turns away. Zidhan closes the notebook.)**

**Zidhan:** "Do what you want. I'll watch from the stands."

**(He walks off the deck. Jaisal stays at the rail. In the lantern light we see his hand tighten on the wood. He doesn't call after him.)**

## Scene 35: The Final — India vs Uruguay (Grit)

**Visual:** The Maracanã again, but this time it has been raining for hours. The pitch is a swamp. The white lines have vanished into brown. The ball, already heavy, is now a wet stone. **It looks exactly like the paddy field in Kerala.** The camera lingers on the mud, and then on Zidhan in the gallery, who sees the same thing. His face softens.

**(Uruguay line up in sky blue. They are not tall. They are not fast. They are *hard.* Their captain, Varela, is a quiet man with a face like a closed door. He looks at India's line and nods, once.)**

**Commentary (radio):** "Uruguay's captain, Obdulio Varela, the Black Chief. He has never lost his temper in a World Cup. He has never lost a tackle either."

**(Kick-off. India open sharply: the back-pass, the wingers, the geometry. In the 9th minute, Antappan throws, Bappu lifts, Kuttappan drives inside, Gopalan dummies, and Raghavan places the ball into the corner. 1-0 India. The stadium thunders. Zidhan, in the gallery, leaps to his feet.)**

**Zidhan:** (Shouting alone, among the crowd) "INDIAAA!"

**(Jaisal, on the touchline, looks up. He sees Zidhan cheering. A flicker of something crosses his face.)**

**(Then Uruguay take over. Not with skill: with *weight.* A hard tackle from behind on Kuttappan: he hits the mud. Play on. A shirt-pull on Raghavan: play on. Varela stands on Gopalan's foot at a throw-in and apologises, courteously. The Indians, whose bodies are used to ropes, nets, and boats, are suddenly in a brawl.)**

**(In the 28th minute, a corner. Uruguay's tallest man rises above Chandu and Pakru, and heads in the equaliser. 1-1. Pakru, who has said "No" to every cross all match, looks at the sky and quietly says "Yes.")**

**(After half-time, the mud thickens. India press, Thomas reads the tell, the trap closes, and the ball slides loose in the swamp. A Uruguayan boot arrives first. The ball sticks in the mud. A scrum. **(Jaisal on the touchline doesn't change anything. He could. He could tell them to foul, to hold, to stand on toes. He raises his hand, opens his mouth, and says nothing. He sticks to his idea. He is, still, a believer.)**

**(Varela wins the ball. Instead of passing, he stops, hand raised, and waits. Time-wasting. The referee watches. The crowd whistles. Varela strolls to the linesman to argue a trivial point, slowly. Two full minutes pass. India's flow dies. The rhythm is gone.)**

**Gopalan:** (Muttering, to Thomas) "He's counting the clock."

**Thomas:** "He's using it."

**Result:** Uruguay 3, India 1. Uruguay champions. India second.

**(Varela walks across the swamp. He stops in front of Gopalan. He puts out a hand. They shake. Varela says something in Spanish. Gopalan, who doesn't speak Spanish, understands it perfectly.)**

**Varela (subtitled):** "You play the way my grandchildren will."

# PHASE 5: THE 360° RETURN

## Scene 36: The Symmetrical Handshake

**Visual:** The Maracanã, emptying. The mud, the rain, the tired ghosts of a final. Jaisal walks the length of the pitch, alone. His T-shirt has lost its colour completely. His shoulders are low. At the barrier in front of the first row of the gallery, a man waits: Zidhan, in a drenched mustard-turned-grey shirt, notebook in his hand, leaning on the rail.

**(Jaisal stops. He looks up. Zidhan looks down. Neither speaks. The rain is almost a mist now.)**

**Jaisal:** (Quietly) "You were right."

**(Zidhan doesn't answer. He doesn't gloat. He doesn't lecture. He only smiles, a small, tired, true smile, the smile of a man who has been in the mud.)**

**(Jaisal reaches up. Zidhan reaches down. Their hands meet over the rail. It is a handshake of acceptance. There is admiration in Jaisal's eyes: for Zidhan's vision, his stubbornness, the years in the mud that Jaisal had walked away from.)**

**Detail:** *Jaisal is on the ground. Zidhan is in the gallery.* This is the geometry of the whole film turned inside out.

**(The watch on Jaisal's wrist glints gold. Its tick grows louder.)**

## Scene 37: The 360° Spin

**Visual:** The camera begins to rotate: a slow, then violent, 360° spin around the clasped hands. The rain streaks into lines. The Maracanã's grey rings whirl by like pages of a flip-book. We see Jaisal's face, Zidhan's face, the sky, the ground, Jaisal, Zidhan.

**(As the camera completes the turn, the black-and-white drains out of the world like water from a basin. A tide of colour floods in from every edge: mud-brown, paddy-green, floodlight white, a red plastic chair, a jersey that is, finally, yellow.)**

**Sound:** The tick explodes into the full roar of a stadium: not the Maracanã, but a small crowd, a village crowd, a few hundred voices shouting in Malayalam, a ref's whistle, a ball hitting a post.

**(We pull back from the clasped hands. The geometry has flipped. Jaisal is in the gallery, in a plastic chair, leaning over the rail. **Zidhan is on the pitch**, in the mud, on the sideline, hand extended up. It's the same handshake. The same expression. Jaisal is admiring. Zidhan is smiling.)**

**Jaisal:** (Almost the same words, but hoarse, from his own mouth) "You were right."

**(Zidhan, grinning, doesn't answer. He just nods once and turns, because the game is not over.)**

## Scene 38: The 2026 Payoff

**Visual:** The paddy field. The mud. The second half of the Kerala 5's final, the real one, in full colour. The scoreboard, a hand-painted wooden board with sliding numbers, reads **1-1.** Seconds remain. Players are spent: Rockstar Boys against Zidhan's boys. Mud-smeared faces. We recognise them: **the keeper (Antappan's face), the captain (Gopalan's), the kid who made the tactical foul (Pakru's), the loud little defender (Velayudhan's), the long passer (Bappu's), the fast one (Kuttappan's), the jumpy one (Karunan's), and the striker (Raghavan's).**

**(In the middle of the pitch, where the dead rat lay, the ball spins free. A Rockstar boy breaks clear on goal. The kid with Pakru's face, the same one who fouled in the first half, steps across him and takes him down: a clean, ruthless tactical foul. Whistle. Yellow card. This time nobody shoves anybody. The kid takes the card with a small nod. Zidhan, at the sideline, nods back. A goal saved. Nobody counts that, except him.)**

**(Zidhan, at the sideline, shouts to Adam: "ADAM! NOW!" Adam, the tall boy, the *Kaalappoott*, who has been waiting his whole life to play, comes on, and in the 14th minute of the second half, from a long throw-in by the keeper, he rises above the Rockstar Boys' tallest defender and heads the ball into the corner.)**

**(The net bulges. The field erupts. Zidhan's kids pile on Adam in a mud-slicked heap. The wooden scoreboard rattles: 2-1. The referee blows three times.)**

**(On the sideline, Akhil sprints in from his Activa and kneels next to a fallen player with a bottle in his hand. It's the new first-aid spray, a full bottle, paid for by the prize money. He presses the nozzle. *PSSSSSST.* A perfect, glorious cloud. Akhil looks at the bottle and then at Zidhan with a face of pure joy.)**

**Akhil:** "It works!"

**Zidhan:** (Laughing) "It's full!"

**(A father, from Part 1, is at the fence, looking at the final score. He turns to his son and says, simply, "We missed the wedding." The son shrugs. They both laugh.)**

**(Jaisal, in the gallery, watches all of it. He watches the mud-smeared kids dancing. He watches Zidhan, at the sideline, a wrung-out man hugging a boy he has coached since the kid was six. The academy is alive. He realises: in 1950 and in 2026 he said the same thing, the same shape of words: *nothing will change.* He was wrong in both. Change is not made of stadiums and politicians; it's made of Zidhan, a notebook, a spray bottle, a kid with a tall head. He was right about the system. He was wrong that you can only wait for it.)**

## Scene 39: The Journey Home

**Visual:** Dusk. The road away from the paddy field, black with wet. Jaisal walks slowly with Adam, who is limping a little, muddy to the hair, a scrape on his knee. Adam is talking non-stop, reliving his goal with both arms.

**Adam:** "...and then I jumped, and I *knew*, Jaisalka, I knew before I touched it—"

**Jaisal:** "I know. I saw."

**(They reach a small house with a veranda. At the door, a woman stands, arms folded, a dishcloth over her shoulder. Shafiq's wife.)**

**Shafiq's Wife:** (Looking at Adam's knee, then at Jaisal, drily) "*Aarelum kayyo kaalo odichu* (somebody's broken a hand or a leg)?"

**Jaisal:** (Smiling) "No, Aunty. Just a scrape. And a goal."

**Adam:** "Amma! I scored the winning goal! I *scored!*"

**(She looks at her son, mud to the eyebrows, shining. She opens her arms, and he walks straight into them. Over Adam's shoulder, she looks at Jaisal. A small nod. This is as much thanks as anyone gets.)**

## Scene 40: The Watch

**Visual:** Night. Jaisal's house. The same quiet room. The fan turning. The same chair. **Grandpa Abu Bakar** sits as he sat at the opening of the film, perfectly still, staring into space. Jaisal enters softly, kneels beside him, and unbuckles the watch from his own wrist. Along the leather strap, nearly invisible in the lamplight, is a thin crust of sea-salt.

**(He lifts Grandpa's wrist and gently fastens the watch on it. The old man doesn't move. But his hand, resting on his knee, turns slightly, as if to feel the weight. A grain of wet sand falls from the buckle onto the floor.)**

**Grandpa:** (Not looking at him, voice soft) "How was the time travel?"

**Jaisal:** "I didn't go to escape it, Grandpa. I went to look at it."

**(A long pause. Grandpa turns his head slowly, for the first time. His eyes are clear.)**

**Grandpa:** "Did you see my father?"

**Jaisal:** (Smiling, his eyes wet) "He asked my name."

**Grandpa:** (Smiling, faintly) "He asks everyone's name."

**(Jaisal laughs, quietly. Grandpa lifts his wrist to his ear and listens to the watch. A long, thoughtful moment. *Tick. Tick.*)**

**Grandpa:** "And?"

**Jaisal:** (Rising, a long breath, looking out of the window at the dark palms) "Good."

**(He turns back and speaks with total sincerity.)**

**Jaisal:** "I want to do it again, Grandpa. Frequently."

**(Grandpa, still holding the watch to his ear, closes his eyes. We hold on his face, at peace.)**

**FADE OUT.**
