## 1. VISUAL & SOUND BIBLE

**2026 (Kerala):** full natural colour, wet green, red mud, harsh noon white. Handheld camera, close and sweaty. Everything a little too crowded.

**1950 (the mind-trip):** high-contrast black & white, golden-hour bloom, vignette edges dissolving into light. Locked-off or slow-gliding camera. **Colour survives in only three things:** the two graphic T-shirts (they drain slowly over the voyage), **fire** (gold), and **the watch** (a faint gold glint always).

**Sound rules (whole film):** no score, no needle-drop. Only what exists in the world: the Azan, the watch tick, wind, surf, oars, rigging, bare feet, coconut thuds, crowd claps, voices. The anthem is sung, never played.

**Anachronism rule:** Jaisal and Zidhan keep their modern words ("press", "load balancing", "midfielder"). The 1950 villagers translate them into their own life-language. That translation is the film's humour and its philosophy.

**Faces echo:** the 1950 villagers are played by the same faces as the 2026 kids. Jaisal recruits, without realising it, the children he already coaches around. The 360° spin reveals it.

# PART 2 — THE CINEMATIC MIND-TRIP (1950)

## Scene 7: The Silent Stadium  ⏱ 2:00  [CORE]

**Visual:** The noise of the 2026 fight vanishes into a single sound: *tick.* The colour bleeds out of the mud, starting at the watch and spreading like ink in water. The light turns amber. The gallery is empty planks and dust. The kids are gone, even Adam: only **one rubber sandal** lies in the mud. Zidhan stands frozen at the pitch edge, **notepad and pen in hand**; his digital watch is blank.

**Jaisal:** (Shouting down, voice echoing) "Zidhan!"

**(Jaisal runs down the gallery steps and grabs Zidhan by the shoulders.)**

**Jaisal:** "Don't panic. We are in 1950."
**Zidhan:** "We were standing in mud two minutes ago."
**Jaisal:** "We still are. Look down."
**Zidhan:** "Why are we here?"
**Jaisal:** (With passion) "Because of the system, Zidhan. The politicians, the broken infrastructure, a spray that goes *pssst* and nothing. Our nurturing is missing. India was invited to the 1950 World Cup and said no. We're going to say yes. If we change history here, the academy will breathe."
**Zidhan:** "You've blamed the system since the semi-final."
**Jaisal:** "Because it *is* the system."
**Zidhan:** "And the system is a place you stopped visiting."
**(Jaisal flinches. He looks away.)**
**Jaisal:** "We aren't the players. We're the coaches. We bring the brains of 2026 to the heart of 1950."
**Zidhan:** "With what? I've only got a notebook."
**Jaisal:** "Pen?"

**(Jaisal tears a clean page from Zidhan's notebook, kneels, and writes on his knee. We see only the last line, in large clear handwriting: *"Our dream is not to destroy others. It is to live happily within them."* He folds it in four.)**

**Jaisal:** "Go to the shore. Find **Sulaiman**, my grandfather's father. He'll be standing where the sea is longest. Tell him the ship must be ready, and we stop at Madras before we sail for Brazil."
**Zidhan:** "And Madras?"
**Jaisal:** "There's a cable going out of Madras. It says India will not participate. It cannot reach Rio."
**Zidhan:** "How do I stop a cable?"
**Jaisal:** "However you can."
**Zidhan:** "Burn the post office?"
**Jaisal:** "If you must."
**Zidhan:** (Pocketing the letter) "I'll do it."
**Jaisal:** "I didn't mean—"
**Zidhan:** "You said *if I must.*"

## Scene 13: The Madras Post Office  ⏱ 2:00  [CORE]

**Visual:** Madras at dawn. A tall red-brick post office, brass counters, rubber stamps, ceiling fans.
